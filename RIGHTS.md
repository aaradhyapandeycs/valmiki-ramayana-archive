# Provenance and reuse decisions

This site distinguishes the ancient Rāmāyaṇa text from the modern presentation
at [valmikiramayan.net](https://valmikiramayan.net/). No permission to mirror
the modern English translation or commentary has been supplied.

| Material | Provenance | Treatment in this project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sanskrit verses and verse locators | Ancient text, as transcribed in the source site's six-book edition | Displayed from recorded source pages. The project checks verse numbering where source labels agree and displays source blocks in order where they disagree. Each chapter records the source URL and response hash. |
| Romanized Sanskrit | Locally generated IAST and links to the original Roman pages | The generated display is identified as IAST and derived from the captured Devanagari. It omits blocks with unsupported glyphs. |
| English translations and word meanings | Modern work credited to Desiraju Hanumanta Rao, K. M. K. Murthy, and contributors | Direct links to original pages. No local copy absent a verified permission or license. |
| M. N. Dutt English translation | Historical public-domain translation from Project Gutenberg 57265, 57826, and 60188 and from the published Critical Edition reader | Read by Dutt’s original section numbers or by Baroda Critical Edition coordinates. Also displayed alongside matched passages in the six-book archive. |
| Prose summaries and commentary | Modern source-site pages, some bearing copyright notices | Direct links only. |
| Fonts, images, audio, PDFs, ZIP downloads | Source-linked files with differing rights and provenance | Direct links only unless independently licensed and checked. |
| Chapter titles and order | Six source chapter indexes | Indexed locally with original links and audit status. |

The source homepage credits Desiraju Hanumanta Rao for Bāla, Araṇya, and
Kiṣkindhā; K. M. K. Murthy for Ayodhyā, Sundara, and Yuddha. The introduction
also names Durga Naaga Devi and Vaasudeva Kishore for Sundara contributions.
The archive preserves the 142-page facsimile of the 1910 C. R. Srinivasa Ayyangar introduction as served by the original site. The PDF is accompanied by a provenance record and SHA-256.

An HTTP 200 response or public availability is **not** a reuse license. A
third-party site's copyright description is not permission from the source
translators. If rights are clarified, record the specific grant and scope
before changing a source-link-only status to hosted content.


The verse reader links to the modern translation at its original source.
The English source-reference index stores only verse references, anchor
names, evidence hashes and audit findings. No modern English translation
text is stored in this index or the generated chapter pages. The separate
Dutt English edition retains its own chapter numbering.
